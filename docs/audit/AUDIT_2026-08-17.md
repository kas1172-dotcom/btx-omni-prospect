## 7. SAMPLE provider vs Prism / Paperless target shape

This is the reshape task you flagged. Below is the current shape, the target Prism / Paperless shape, and what should change so nothing has to be re-plumbed at cutover.

### 7a. Current SAMPLE shape (`providers/sample/environment.py`)

`SampleEnvironment` holds fifteen tuples/dicts: `accounts`, `facilities`, `commercial_contexts`, `paperless_accounts`, `quotes`, `identity_map`, `scenario_accounts`, `crm_contexts`, `public_signals`, `scoring_inputs`, `intelligence_events`, `matching_components`, `matching_quotes`, `research_mappings`, `researched_accounts`, `watch_profiles`, `public_facilities`, `rich_scenarios`.

It is built once at process start (`build_sample_environment()`), lives in `PocRuntime.sample`, and never touches Postgres. Twelve companies get curated scenarios. Every commercial fact is stamped with `Provenance("poc-simulated-btx-context", ...)` and `data_mode=SAMPLE, synthetic=True`.

Shape of the key commercial dataclasses (already correct as models, wrong as persistence and grain):

**`CommercialContext`** (per account-BU): `account_id`, `business_unit`, `currency`, `ttm_revenue_minor`, `ttm_bookings_minor`, `customer_segment`, `end_market`, `platform_program`, `part_number`, `last_booking_date`, `last_order_date`, `monthly_history` (tuple of `MonthlyCommercialHistory`), `last_crm_activity_date`, `intelligence_evidence_ids`, `jamie_validation_required`.

**`CommercialQuote`** (per Paperless quote): `id`, `account_id`, `business_unit`, `status` (OPEN/WON/LOST/EXPIRED), `quoted_at`, `value_minor`, `currency`, `contact_id`, `facility_id`, `part_family`, `quote_to_book_evidence_ids`.

**`PaperlessAccount`**: `id`, `canonical_account_id`, `name`. **Very thin.** Missing everything Paperless exposes about the account itself.

**`SampleCrmContext`**: `account_id`, `company_id`, `owner_id`, `contact_role_families`, `deal_ids`, `activity_ids`. **Extremely thin** and diverges from the `domain/crm.py` model (which has fully realized `CrmCompany`, `CrmContact`, `CrmDeal`, `CrmActivity`). The sample provider never actually populates the richer CRM dataclasses.

### 7b. Target commercial-data shape (Prism / BTX data lake)

**What Prism actually is** (per the Alan call): Prism is BTX's own BI application, a PowerBI-fronted analytics navigator built by BTX on top of a normalized data lake. The data lake is BTX's crown-jewel work: it aggregates and normalizes across 8 different ERPs. Prism was BTX's first application on top of that lake; there's room for more. The console reads (eventually) from the same lake, not from Prism.

Implication: there is no "Prism API" or "Prism vendor SDK." The source of truth is BTX's normalized commercial data lake. What Alan can share, when the time comes, is either a controlled sample extract (~100 accounts is what he offered), a schema description of the lake tables/views, or eventually direct authorized access via a formal contractor engagement in the Gemini Enterprise sandbox. All three paths remain open.

**Grain the console needs, regardless of transport:**
- **customer × business unit × month** for revenue and bookings.
- **per-transaction** for orders (needed for overdue-order alerts and reorder-gap analysis).
- **customer × business unit** for TTM aggregates, `last_booking_date`, `last_order_date`.
- **facility-level** for the capacity-estimate signal Jamie showed you (which is derived from production history, not a measured field).

**Columns the console needs, expressed source-agnostically** (real column names come from the lake schema when Alan shares it):
- Customer identity keyed to a canonical master (BTX's normalized customer_id across ERPs).
- Business unit.
- Time grain (month for aggregates, transaction date for orders).
- Revenue minor and bookings minor.
- Orders: order_id, part_number, program, ship-to, promised date, actual ship date, status, quantity, line amount.
- Optional but valuable: end_market, customer_segment, platform/program, part_number aggregate.

**Reshape action for SAMPLE:** keep the current `CommercialContext` dataclass as the console-facing shape, but split the sample provider's storage into a `providers/lake_sample/` module that models rows in long form (`customer_bu_month`), plus a separate `orders_transaction` shape. This makes the SAMPLE fixture look structurally like what an ETL from the lake would produce, without pretending we know the exact BTX column names. When Alan sends real data, the ingester maps his columns onto the console-facing dataclasses and nothing downstream (scoring, alerts, matching) has to change.

The point is not to lock the SAMPLE to a hypothetical Prism schema; it's to lock the SAMPLE to a shape that mirrors "normalized commercial warehouse" so the eventual mapping is mechanical.

### 7d. Target HubSpot shape (bidirectional)

**Framing.** BTX's HubSpot portal, its custom properties, its pipelines, its owner mappings, and its actual object model are not available to this POC. The HubSpot MCP currently connected in this session is Kapil's personal HubSpot, used to prove that the console can read from and write to HubSpot with human confirmation on writes. Fidelity to BTX's specific property schema is deferred to when BTX's HubSpot is authorized.

**HubSpot is bidirectional.** The console reads from HubSpot (owner, deal stage, last activity, contacts, associations) and writes back to HubSpot (Prism-derived revenue/bookings as company properties, Paperless-derived quote status as deal properties, console-driven actions logged as activities, deal-stage advances when workflows complete, contact-role enrichment when the relationship matrix identifies shared contacts). Every field the console touches needs a field-ownership policy:
- **HubSpot-owned:** seller edits in HubSpot, console reads only. Example: `hubspot_owner_id`, deal notes, contact preferences.
- **Console-owned:** console writes from lake/Paperless data, HubSpot displays. Example: `btx_ttm_revenue`, `btx_last_quote_status`, `btx_open_quote_value`.
- **Hybrid:** console proposes, seller confirms in HubSpot. Example: `dealstage` (console suggests advance, seller confirms).

Encode this as a `field_ownership_policy` map keyed by HubSpot property name. Every console write stamps a `source: console-enriched` marker (via a custom HubSpot property or activity metadata) so a seller can always tell what came from where.

What you can shape against today (per the HubSpot public v3 API, which any portal exposes):
- **companies (default properties only):** `hs_object_id`, `name`, `domain`, `industry`, `hubspot_owner_id`, `lifecyclestage`, `annualrevenue`, `numberofemployees`, `city`, `state`, `country`.
- **contacts (default):** `hs_object_id`, `firstname`, `lastname`, `email`, `phone`, `jobtitle`, `hubspot_owner_id`, `associatedcompanyid`.
- **deals (default):** `hs_object_id`, `dealname`, `dealstage`, `pipeline`, `amount`, `closedate`, `hubspot_owner_id`, `hs_deal_stage_probability`.
- **owners (default):** `hubspot_owner_id`, `firstName`, `lastName`, `email`.
- **engagements (default):** `hs_object_id`, `hs_activity_type`, `hs_engagement_timestamp`, `hubspot_owner_id`.

These are the fields every HubSpot portal has out of the box. They are safe to use for POC read-and-write demonstration without knowing BTX's custom schema. When BTX HubSpot is connected, expect additional custom properties (industry-specific fields, BU-specific fields, custom deal stages, custom pipelines) that will need discovery via `HubSpot.search_properties` before the sample-to-real swap is honest.

**Reshape action for the POC:** in the sample provider, populate `CrmCompany`, `CrmContact`, `CrmDeal`, `CrmActivity` with the default properties above, marked `SAMPLE` and `synthetic=True` in provenance. This gives the frontend real-shaped data to render and gives both directions of the HubSpot exchange something to work against. Custom BTX property mapping is a separate P2 task once BTX HubSpot is in play. The field-ownership policy above is the artifact that migrates cleanly to BTX HubSpot.

## 8. The relationship matrix (new scope)

Per your addition to scope: "the relationship matrix I've designed in figma - this should allow the user to see contacts and orders that are connected to other clients/potential clients."

**Figma coverage.** One full desktop concept frame `concept-relationship-network` (1509x966), with sub-frames: `Relationship Intelligence — graph-led exploration` (graph view), `Account Portfolio Matrix` (tabular view), `List Matrix`, `graph-canvas`, controls (`Fit network`), and named example content (ASML Holding, NXE:3800 Lithography System). It reads as two coordinated views on the same relationship data: a network graph and a portfolio matrix.

**Backend coverage.** None. No edges table, no graph traversal API, no shared-contact / shared-program / shared-order query. `CanonicalAccount` has `parent_account_id` and that's the entire relationship model.

**What "connected" actually means for BTX (from your scope + Figma):**
- Two accounts share a contact (same person, different companies).
- Two accounts share a program (both suppliers to Lockheed on the same NASA CLPS contract).
- Two accounts share an order-of-record (subcontractor and prime on the same BOM).
- Two accounts share a facility or ship-to (same physical location, common in aerospace tiers).
- Two accounts share a parent (Lockheed Space and Lockheed Missiles roll up to the same top).
- Two accounts are competitors in a make/buy decision on the same program.

Each edge type has different data provenance and different confidence.

**Proposed data model** (concise, ready to code):

```
account_relationship_edges (
  id, from_account_id, to_account_id,
  edge_type,  -- SHARED_CONTACT | SHARED_PROGRAM | SHARED_ORDER | SHARED_FACILITY | PARENT_CHILD | COMPETITOR_ON_PROGRAM
  strength,   -- 0..100 or enum WEAK/MODERATE/STRONG
  evidence_ids, provenance_source, data_mode, evidence_state,
  first_observed_at, last_observed_at,
  derived_from_object_type, derived_from_object_id,  -- e.g. contact_id=X, program_id=Y
  bidirectional  -- boolean; SHARED_CONTACT is bidirectional, PARENT_CHILD is not
)
```

**Derivation rules** (deterministic, not model-based):
- `SHARED_CONTACT`: distinct `crm_contacts.email` (or a normalized `firstname+lastname+phone`) associated with more than one `crm_companies.account_id`. Strength scales with role_family relevance (procurement > engineering > operations).
- `SHARED_PROGRAM`: distinct `programs.id` referenced by `commercial_quotes` or `intelligence_signals` for more than one account.
- `SHARED_ORDER`: distinct `orders.program_id + orders.part_number` present across accounts (subtier and prime).
- `SHARED_FACILITY`: distinct `facilities` with matching `latitude+longitude` (rounded) or matching `city+region+facility_name` normalized.
- `PARENT_CHILD`: from `accounts.parent_account_id`.
- `COMPETITOR_ON_PROGRAM`: two accounts with `commercial_quotes.program_id` matching but different `status` outcomes (WON vs LOST on same program).

**API surface** (proposed):
- `GET /accounts/{id}/relationships` returns edge set with 1-hop neighbors and strength scores.
- `GET /accounts/{id}/relationships?depth=2` for 2-hop network for graph rendering.
- `GET /relationships/matrix?industry=defense&edge_type=SHARED_CONTACT` for the Portfolio Matrix view.

**Postgres is fine for this.** No graph database needed at POC scale. The edges are derived, not authoritative, so a materialized view refreshed on write to `crm_contacts`, `commercial_quotes`, `orders`, or `facilities` is the natural implementation. If the graph ever exceeds a few thousand accounts with dense edges, Postgres recursive CTEs still handle 2-3 hop traversals cleanly.

**Frontend rendering** (matches the Figma):
- **Graph view.** MapLibre is already loaded; use a lightweight canvas graph library like `sigma.js` (~50KB) or plain SVG with a small force-directed layout (`d3-force` at ~15KB). Skip heavyweight libraries like cytoscape.js unless you actually need clustering.
- **Matrix view.** Native table with a heatmap intensity per edge_type. No new dependency.
